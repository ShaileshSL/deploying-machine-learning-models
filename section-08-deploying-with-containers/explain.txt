Why Docker Still Matters
1. Environment Consistency
Railway abstracts infrastructure, but your app still runs on their containers behind the scenes.

Docker lets you define exactly what your runtime environment looks like (Python version, system libraries, OS packages).

Without Docker, you rely on Railway’s defaults — which may differ from your local dev setup.

2. Portability
Docker images can run anywhere: Railway, AWS, GCP, Azure, or even on-prem servers.

If you ever migrate away from Railway, Docker ensures your app runs identically elsewhere.

3. Reproducibility
CI/CD pipelines often use Docker to guarantee builds are reproducible.

Instead of “works on my machine,” you get a frozen environment defined in a Dockerfile.

4. Complex Dependencies
If your app needs system-level libraries (e.g., libpq for Postgres, ffmpeg for media), Docker makes sure they’re baked into the image.

Railway alone may not provide those unless you configure them manually.

5. Security & Isolation
Docker isolates your app from the host system.

You can apply security patches by rebuilding the base image, independent of Railway’s environment.
